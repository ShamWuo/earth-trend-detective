1. Problem Framing: 1 City, 1 User, 1 Decision

City & User: Denver, CO (39.74°N, -104.99°W, 1,609 m) — a K-12 School Microgrid Operator running a 250 kWdc rooftop PV array and a 350 kWh LFP battery storage system.

Actionable Decision (23:00 MST): Predict tomorrow's daily solar irradiance (ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, kWh/m²/day) to decide whether to pre-charge the battery overnight at off-peak rates ($0.075/kWh, $26.25/cycle) in order to prevent expensive daytime peak grid deficits during low-solar storm days (< 3.5 kWh/m²/d at $0.280/kWh; $0.205/kWh spread).

2. 7-Node Climate Graph, Evening Advection & q* Split

Daily satellite averages smear Front Range storm approaches that traverse the 93–156 km corridor in 2–4 hours. A 7-Node Front Range Climate Graph connects Denver (Target) to six upwind stations—Silverthorne/Divide (W), Fort Collins (NNW), Cheyenne, WY (N), Greeley (NE), Limon (E), and Colorado Springs (S)—weighted by directional wind advection wj(t) = max(0, cos(WDt − θDenver→j)). We join NASA POWER Daily (2019–2026, 2,870 days) with Open-Meteo Hourly (18:00–23:00 MST, 100.0% coverage: cloud, RH, wind, ΔP23h–18h).

Under asymmetric TOU tariffs, Bayes risk sets the zero-tuning Newsvendor critical fractile q* = 0.075 / 0.280 ≈ 0.268 (dispatch iff Q0.268 < 3.50). Chronological split (zero random shuffling): Train 2019–2023 (1,824d) → Val 2024 (366d: $1,398 → $1,198 → $1,133/yr) → Unseen Test 2025–2026 (606d).
